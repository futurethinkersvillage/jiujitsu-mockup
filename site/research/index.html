Fame didn't help: 10th Planet HQ scored 22. The nearest club to Clearwater, Grizzly Jiu Jitsu in Barriere (about 60 km), is social media only.

Patterns of the top 10%

  1. One first-class button, worded the same, in the hero, mid-page and footer.
  2. Split visitors early: kids by age band, adults, beginners.
  3. Explain the first class plainly: what to wear, waivers, parents can watch.
  4. Named coaches with ranks, certifications and lineage.
  5. Testimonials with real names, especially parents.
  6. A schedule and prices you can read at a glance, including federation fees.

What everyone does (so we don't)

  • Red and black on about 13 of 17 sites. No one uses green, earth or nature colours.
  • All-caps condensed headlines (Anton, Oswald, DIN Condensed). Zero sites use a serif or Japanese type.
  • Superlatives like “#1” and “world champion instructors”, which a new club can't claim and shouldn't.
  • Dark, high-contrast athlete photos that read as intimidating to parents. Stock photos that erode trust.

Where Clearwater can stand out

  • Own the place: Wells Gray colours, real members in natural light, mountain views.
  • “Two arts, one mat”: tell the shared Kanō → Maeda → Gracie story. No regional club does this well.
  • Publish prices, family rates and any Judo BC fee. Link KidSport and Jumpstart.
  • Teach on the site: Japanese terms, belt pathways, etiquette, “we teach falling first”. This is what the poster set does.
  • Visitors page for resort guests, seasonal workers and fire crews.
  • Rural practicalities: nearby communities, carpooling, winter driving, school-friendly times.
  • Posters with QR codes and a printable first-class coupon.
  • An honest small-club voice: certified, safe and welcoming, not superlative.

Mistakes seen on competitor sites

Lorem ipsum testimonials, booking widgets showing 2 AM classes, broken map embeds and leftover template links. Every placeholder on our preview sites is clearly marked TBC for that reason.
